# The reference planner (#43)

How the reference engine chooses each Arrangement in the [golden data](README.md), which Auto-map (`apps/web/src/lib/engine/`) follows term by term.

The reference planner follows the unlock rule in [ADR 0004](../../docs/adr/0004-fart-only-rendering.md) and [ADR 0006](../../docs/adr/0006-octave-moves.md). `melody_fit()` gives Pitch Coverage and the Arrangement in one search; `plan()` turns an Unlocked Song's Arrangement into notes, and raises `SongLocked` for a locked one.

- **Allowed moves.** Each distinct Song pitch gets its own Melodic Recording, and a Recording plays at most one pitch. Its shift (target minus Recording Pitch) is at most 3 semitones either way, or at most 3 from ±12 (an Octave Move, up or down on equal terms; ADR 0006 records why). Every transposition from −36 to +24 is tried.
- **Played raw (ADR 0012).** The shift and the Octave Move decide only which Recording may play a pitch and what it costs. The renderer plays every fart unshifted, at its own pitch and length, so an Octave Move plays its fart an octave from the note.
- **Long enough (ADR 0011).** A Recording covers a pitch only if it lasts at least `NOTE_FILL` (1.0) × the pitch's longest note: `gesture length ≥ NOTE_FILL × max(dur × 60 / bpm − 0.025)` (`fills()`). Before ADR 0012 the gesture length was scaled by 2^(−shift/12), so an Octave Move down doubled a Recording. A pitch no Recording is long enough for stays uncovered, and `missing[]` says how long a fart it needs.
- **Ranking.** Arrangements compare by the fewest uncovered pitches (the most Pitch Coverage), then the fewest Octave Moves, then the lowest cost, where costs within 1e-9 are equal. Then the Recording ids, read in ascending Song pitch order, lexicographically smallest, with an uncovered pitch sorting after every id. Then the lower transposition. Sorting the library before the solver isn't enough, because a different solver can break the same tie differently.
- **Cost**, per covered pitch, summed in ascending pitch order:
  - weight × move cost. The weight is the summed duration in beats of the notes at that pitch. The move cost per beat is |shift|^1.5 within 3 semitones, or 6 + |shift ∓ 12|^1.5 for an Octave Move, so an Octave Move always costs more than any real-pitch move (3^1.5 = 5.2).
  - for each note at that pitch that sounds for less than the Recording's `shortest_note_s`: 8, plus 400 per second it falls short. A note sounds for `dur × 60 / bpm − 0.025` seconds, or for the Recording's gesture length when that's shorter.
  - for each note at that pitch, 20 × the share of its sounding time that comes before the Recording settles (#92): `20 × min(sounds, settle_s) / sounds`, with `sounds` as above. A short note on a fart that scoops or glides into its pitch costs up to 20; the same fart on a long note costs little.
  - 30 × the seconds of silence the Recording leaves in that pitch's notes (#205): `30 × Σ max(0, (dur × 60 / bpm − 0.025) − gesture length)`, summed over the notes first and then multiplied. A long note prefers a long Recording.

What changed from the planner before #43: no Recording is reused (the reuse penalty of 2 is gone), no shift goes beyond 3 semitones except with an Octave Move (the old shift cost allowed up to 12), the too-short cost grows with how far short a note falls and counts the Recording's own length, and ties are broken by the rule above. #92 added the settle cost, #205 the silence cost, and ADR 0011 (#221) the length rule, which decides whether a Recording may cover a pitch at all. ADR 0012 (#226) made every length the Recording's own, since farts now play unshifted. `golden_cost.py` must change with any cost term; the export fails until they agree. `recordings/` and `songs/` don't depend on the planner.
