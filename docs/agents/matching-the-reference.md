# Matching the reference

What the web app's port in `src/lib/analysis/` and `src/lib/engine/` must reproduce of the reference engine (`tools/melody-lab/`), and why. How the two are compared is in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../../ARCHITECTURE.md#how-the-web-app-stays-matched-to-the-reference). Beside the golden data, `fixtures/golden/files.md` has the contour's smoothing steps and the Recording Pitch estimate, `port-tests.md` how close a port must come, and `planner.md` the planner's ranking and costs, which Auto-map follows term by term.

## Recording Analysis

- **Numerics** (`numeric.ts`): V8's `2 ** x` is one ulp off the C library's `pow` on 43 of pYIN's 519 bins, and `Math.log2` off its `log2` on 3 of the grid's `midi_to_hz` values, so `exp2` (a double-double Taylor series) and `log2` (one Newton step from V8's estimate) are correctly rounded. `pairwiseSum` is `np.sum`, `roundHalfEven` is `np.round`, Auto-map's `pythonSum` is Python 3.12's Neumaier `sum()`, and `compareIds` orders ids by code point, as Python strings do.
- **pYIN** (`pyin.ts`) is librosa 1.0.0's `pyin` (45 to 900 Hz, frame 2048, hop 256, librosa's other defaults), step by step: autocorrelation by FFT with n = 4096 (`next_fast_len(4095)`, each real transform done as a half-size complex one), the cumulative mean normalized difference (librosa zeroes the first energy term), parabolic interpolation, troughs weighted by the Beta(2, 18) threshold prior (for integer shapes, I_x(2, 18) = 1 − (1−x)^19 − 19x(1−x)^18) and Boltzmann prior 2, then one Viterbi step per frame over transitions of probability at least 1e-4. Recording Pitches sit on its 0.1-semitone grid (MIDI x.x259), so its bins, half-to-even rounding and ties must match: a later, longer-period trough overwrites its bin, and the backtrack starts from the first maximum. `evaluate.py` reads renders back up to 1200 Hz instead.
- **Resampling** (`resample.ts`) stands in for soxr's `soxr_hq`, `librosa.load`'s default: a Kaiser-windowed sinc with soxr's figures for that quality (20 bits, 120.4 dB rejection, passband to 1 − 0.05 / TO_3dB(rejection)), cut off midway through the transition band, `ceil(n × 44100 / rate)` samples rounded to float32. An integer rate computes one coefficient table per phase (147 for 48 kHz). It isn't soxr to the bit; the pitch grid absorbs the difference.
- **The fart** (`noteSegment` in `trimming.ts`) is the reference's `note_segment()` (ADR 0016): the take with only the silence before and after it trimmed, and its first burst. Swift's `Trimming.noteSegment` stopped at the first burst. It ends on the 50 ms RMS envelope, which smooths fast flutter away, after the last frame within 30 dB of its peak, so a second burst is kept. The pitch end is on the same envelope: the first slow re-attack (a dip of more than 6 dB below the running maximum, then a rise of more than 4 dB, more than 60 ms after the start), else the end. Recording Analysis reads the pitch from the start to the pitch end only, so a second burst can't blur it; Songs play to the end, every burst.
- **The Recording Pitch** (`estimate` in `recording-pitch.ts`): when the first 450 ms of the contour give no pitch, it reads the 450 ms from where the contour starts, for notes that start late (#282, ADR 0017).
- **A Trim** (`trimBounds` in `trimming.ts`) is the reference's `trim_bounds()` (ADR 0019): the handles are cut at whole samples of the 44.1 kHz audio, then `noteSegment` finds the fart between them, so a Trim always lies within its handles and its onset lands on the beat as an untrimmed fart's does.
- **Audition and the settle time** (`audition.ts`, `variable-resample.ts`) read the gesture back with its pitch held: scipy's `butter`, `filtfilt` (odd extension, `lfilter_zi` state) and numpy's complex division, then Catmull-Rom reads. Only Recording Analysis does this; Songs play farts raw (ADR 0012).
- **`ANALYSIS_VERSION`** (`version.ts`) goes up whenever Recording Analysis's answers change: 2 added the settle time (#116), 3 kept the attack in the contour (#208), 4 is the whole fart (ADR 0016), and 5 the second pitch window (ADR 0017). An analysis stored with an older version, or none, is analyzed again on the next open (`upgradeAnalyses`). It has its own module so the app reads it without loading Recording Analysis.

## Songs, Pads and Performances

- **The renderer** (`renderer.ts`) is `render()`: `np.linspace` fades, each note's level set over its first 0.25 s, a downbeat where beat ÷ beats per bar is within 1e-6 of a whole bar, and the tanh limiter. Where a note ends as written is summed in seconds (`n.start × spb + n.dur × spb`), as the reference sums it; summing `(n.start + n.dur) × spb` breaks the bit-for-bit match. The next lead-in is held back until a fart played past its written end stops. Where the reference would fail it keeps the Swift port's behavior, which no golden Song reaches: a note past the end of the mix is clipped, and fades on a note shorter than the fade-in are clamped.
- **The lead-in** (`leadInStart` in `render-source.ts`) is the reference's `lead_in_start()` (#287). On the 5 ms envelope the gesture's start comes from, it walks back from the attack's frame (the first within 12 dB of the peak) over every frame within 30 dB of the peak and over quieter gaps of up to 50 ms, and starts 8 ms before the earliest, as the gesture starts 8 ms before the attack. A sound before a longer gap isn't the fart's: on the owner's room-noisy phone takes it was room noise, up to 2 s of it (#295). It's at most 0.25 s, which keeps `009`'s 0.215 s G3 opening and no more room noise. With a Trim, the lead-in is found between the handles too, as the reference's `trimmed()` does, so nothing before the start handle plays. Songs play the lead-in in the rest before the note's beat, never in the gap after a note nor before the Song's start, and set a note's level from its attack, not the quiet lead-in.
- **Auto-map** (`auto-map.ts`): `neededSeconds` is never negative, so a note shorter than the gap before the next no longer breaks Melody Fit (#332). `coveredCount` counts what Auto-map covers without arranging, for Compose's 1,000 rows (#323).
- **A Pad's sound** (`pads.ts`, #298): one rule for where Songs and Pads start a fart. A Pad starts where a Song starts the fart (`leadInStart` on the take at 44.1 kHz, from `noteSegment`'s attack), so room noise before it doesn't make a tap sound late, and ends just after its last 10 ms window within 30 dB of its loudest, so a second fart stays (owner, 2026-10-30, #275). A take with no sound is kept whole. Finding the start of a take at another rate takes a resampling, about 10 ms a second of audio, so Pads are cut in the render worker. Every Pad peaks at 0.5 (−6 dBFS, `PAD_PEAK`), so two at once can't clip: a fixed gain per sound, as ADR 0004 allows. Since #284 a take is saved peaking at 0.89, so a Pad plays it about 5 dB under the Recordings tab. Fades are linear, 3 ms in and up to 30 ms out.
- **A Performance** (`render-performance.ts`, #278, ADR 0017): each tap plays its Recording as a Pad plays it, raw from its start, at the tap's time to the nearest sample at 44.1 kHz, and the taps add up; nothing loops, stretches or limits. The one gain on the whole mix is 1, unless the taps together would pass full scale (0x7fff and 0x8000, what a 16-bit WAV holds), and then it puts the mix's peak there rather than clip. A Performance uploads with its Toot, so it's kept small (`performance.ts`): each Recording's id once, and each tap as `[seconds from the first tap, index]`, to the millisecond, for at most 30 s and `MAX_TAPS` taps.

## Around the engine

- **Decoding the fixtures** (`apps/web/scripts/decode-audio-file.ts`, which the golden tests and the scripts share) reads the fixtures as libsndfile does: MP3 through mpg123 in gapless mode, so frame counts match exactly, with the channel count from the first frame header (mpg123-decoder doubles a mono stream); WAV scaled by 1/32768; channels averaged in float32.
- **The Web Workers** share `analysis/worker-requests.ts`: one worker per kind, requests answered by id, and a failed worker rejects what's pending and is replaced on the next request. The analysis worker answers Recording Analysis and a Trim (`findTrim`, which resamples the whole take to 44.1 kHz first); the render worker answers a Song, a Sound Pad Performance (`renderPerformanceInWorker`) and a Pad's sound (`padSoundInWorker`). Recording Analysis is sent a compact copy of the samples, since cloning a view copies its whole buffer, and each render's channels are transferred back.
- **A Trim's tolerances** (`src/server/library-input.ts`): a Trim's handles may reach 0.01 s past the take's `seconds` (`TRIM_TOLERANCE_SECONDS`), since the browser measured those at the take's own sample rate and the handles at the 44.1 kHz analysis reads, so a handle on the last sample can land a fraction of a millisecond past it. Its fart may reach 0.001 s past its handles (`SAMPLE_TOLERANCE`), since `trimBounds` cuts the handles at whole samples, so the fart can start half a sample before the start handle. Any MIDI note is accepted as a Placed Pitch, not only the review's C2 to B4, so a later release can widen the keys and this Worker still keeps its uploads.
- **Search** (`src/lib/catalog/search-key.ts`) compares names in lower case, without accents or apostrophes, "&" as "and", and every run of other punctuation and spaces as one space, as the catalog tools' `pdmx.normalize`, which matched the names to their scores (#323).
